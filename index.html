01 · The Three Streams

One wellbore, three supply chains

A modern Utica well produces crude oil and natural gas and NGLs simultaneously through the same wellbore. Above ground a separator splits them by density, and from that point each stream lives in its own world.

  • Crude oil — liquid C5+ molecules. Cannot be used directly; goes by truck or pipeline to a refinery (Toledo, Detroit, Gulf Coast). Priced off WTI at Cushing; Utica condensate is light (60+ API) and trades $5–10/bbl below WTI.
  • Natural gas — pure methane. Compressed into a transmission pipeline (NEXUS, Rover, REX) and on to power plants, home furnaces, factories, or LNG export. Priced off Henry Hub; Ohio basis −$0.30 to −$0.40/Mcf.
  • NGLs — ethane, propane, butane, isobutane, natural gasoline. Vapor at wellhead, liquid after a cryogenic chill to −120 °F. Trades as a basket at ~30–35% of WTI; ethane is the cheapest component, natural gasoline the most valuable.

Mental model: BOE is an energy conversion (6 Mcf gas = 1 boe), but at today's prices 1 boe of gas is worth ~18% of 1 boe of oil. A "1,000 boe/d at 50% gas" well has revenue 90% tilted to oil.

02 · From Drill Bit to Pump

The 8 steps of a Tuscarawas well

  1. Lease — sign with mineral-rights owners. ~$5K/acre signing bonus + 18–22% royalty.
  2. Permit & unitize — Ohio DNR + combine leases into a unit big enough for 3-mile laterals.
  3. Pad & roads — bulldoze 5-acre site, gravel, containment. ~$1M/pad.
  4. Drill vertical — straight down ~8,000 ft to top of Point Pleasant.
  5. Drill lateral — curve 90° and drill horizontally ~15,500 ft inside the 100-ft target rock.
  6. Casing & cement — steel pipe + cement to isolate from groundwater.
  7. Frac — 15–20M gallons water + 30–40M lbs sand + 0.5% chemicals at 9,000 psi across 50 stages.
  8. Flowback & first oil — 1–3 weeks for water to flow back, then hydrocarbons.

Spud-to-first-production: 90–120 days. Total D&C cost: ~$11.4M. A typical pad bundles 3–6 wells drilled with one rig, then all fracked together.

04 · Behind-the-Meter Thesis

Why hyperscalers pay 4× for on-site gas

PJM interconnection takes 4–7 years. Microsoft, Google, AWS, and Meta need 24/7 power now. So they're building gas-fired plants directly on data center campuses, fueled by gas wells just down the road.

A 100 MW IT load (PUE 1.2) draws 120 MW continuous, sized to a 150 MW CCGT (~80% availability). At a 7,000 Btu/kWh heat rate, that burns ~25 MMcf/day — the output of 7 wells at peak, or ~13 wells at stabilized year-2 rates. Sold as pipeline gas at $2.30/Mcf those volumes return ~$57K/day. Run through turbines and leased to a hyperscaler at $80/MWh they return ~$288K/day — a 4× premium on the same molecules.

The catch: ~$1B+ of incremental capex (CCGT $150–200M, data center $800M–1.2B), 36–48 month build, and a continuous drilling treadmill (45–50 wells over 20 years, ~$510–570M of D&C alone) just to keep the turbines fed. The hyperscaler lease is a gas-supply obligation as much as a real-estate deal.

05 · Pricing & Realized Price

Why realized price is always lower than benchmark

  • Quality differential — Utica oil is light (60+ API) and sells $5–10/bbl below WTI; Ohio gas $0.20–0.40 below Henry Hub.
  • Transportation — pipeline tariffs and gathering fees: $1–3/bbl on oil, $0.20–0.50/Mcf on gas.
  • Processing — NGL recovery costs $5–9/bbl through cryogenic plant + fractionator.
  • Basis — regional supply/demand. Permian gas occasionally trades negative when pipelines fill. Ohio basis is more stable but discounted.

What's driving Apr 2026: WTI elevated (~$100, US-Iran tensions, OPEC+ discipline), Henry Hub depressed (~$2.68, mild weather, full storage), NGLs at ~32% of WTI, Ohio basis tightening as central-Ohio data-center build-out adds in-basin demand.

06 · Glossary

Vocabulary you'll hear in every meeting

API gravityDensity of crude. >40° = light. Utica is ~60° (very light).
Basis differentialLocal price minus benchmark hub. Ohio gas ~ HH minus $0.30.
BOEBarrel of oil equivalent. 6 Mcf gas = 1 BOE on energy basis.
D&CDrilling & completion. ~$11.4M for an EOG Utica well.
Decline curveHow well production drops over time. Shale wells decline ~50% in yr 1.
EUREstimated Ultimate Recovery — total volume over life. Utica: 1.5–2.0 MMboe.
GORGas-to-oil ratio. Tells you whether a well is oil-rich or gas-rich.
IP30 / IP90Initial Production rate averaged over first 30 / 90 days.
LateralHorizontal section of well. Modern Utica laterals: 15,000+ ft.
Mcf / MMcf / BcfThousand / million / billion cubic feet of natural gas.
MMBtuMillion British Thermal Units. 1 Mcf ≈ 1.024 MMBtu.
NGLNatural Gas Liquids — ethane, propane, butane, isobutane, natural gasoline.
PUEPower Usage Effectiveness. Best-in-class new-build = 1.2.
RoyaltyMineral-rights owner's share. Typically 18–22% in Ohio.
Volatile oil windowSweet spot of Utica producing oil + NGLs + gas in commercial volume.
WTI / BrentOil benchmarks. WTI = US (Cushing OK); Brent = North Sea.
