THE SITUATION

Landowner with optionality

  • Location. Volatile-oil window of the Utica/Point Pleasant — immediately adjacent to producing EOG-comparable acreage in Carroll and Harrison counties.
  • Status today. Undeveloped — held but not drilled, leased but not built upon. Pad-ready and clean.
  • Optionality. Each acre can be sold outright, drilled for hydrocarbons, host a power plant, or anchor a behind-the-meter data-center campus.
  • Decision space. The right answer depends on capital available, risk tolerance, and how one reads the market.
→
THE MARKET

Why the window is open now

  • Oil prices elevated. WTI near $100 on OPEC+ discipline and geopolitics — a multi-year tailwind for upstream economics.
  • Data-center build-out. Central Ohio is absorbing every megawatt available — Microsoft, Google, AWS, Meta all expanding in-state.
  • Behind-the-meter premium. PJM interconnect runs 4–7 years; hyperscalers pay ~4× for on-site gas-fired power that can come online faster.
  • Selling power to the grid. AEP-Ohio LMPs lifted by AI demand; spark-spreads for new gas-fired capacity widen as coal retires.
  • Gas basis tightening. In-basin power demand is finally absorbing stranded Appalachian gas — the discount to Henry Hub is structurally narrowing.
→
THE QUESTION

Which path, and how to fund it?

Each path carries a different capital commitment, time horizon, and risk profile. The right answer depends on commodity prices, lease rates, and how the market plays out — it’s a numbers question.

This application helps answer that question. It’s a working financial model that runs all four paths end-to-end — capex, revenue, financing, taxes, distributions — and ranks them by Owner NPV under whatever assumptions you set.

    Why this matters

    The decline is the treadmill

    Shale wells lose 50–70% of their rate in year one. A well count sized off IP collapses fast — you don't get a fixed fleet, you get a continuous drilling program to hold deliverability under the plant. The table above front-loads year 1 to reach plateau and then settles into a steady maintenance cadence by roughly year 8, when the aging fleet's accumulated tail starts carrying most of the base load.

    The single assumption that swings the answer is per-well IP. In Belmont/Monroe dry-gas Utica with IP30s of 15+ MMcf/d, a 500 MW plant runs on 6–8 wells out of the gate. On the oilier western edge (Tuscarawas), associated gas drops to 2–4 MMcf/d per well and the year-1 count balloons to 25–45 wells — at which point a Nexus firm-transport blend stops being a hedge and becomes the supply backbone.

    The competitive process

    A managed, advisor-led process — never a bilateral negotiation. We run it end-to-end to manufacture competition and drive a market-clearing price. Each stage narrows the field while sharpening terms:

    1. 140+ parties
      Tease & qualify
      Anonymous one-page teaser to the buyer universe; execute NDAs with parties that lean in.
    2. 210–12 in
      Open the data room
      Share the package — CIM, financial model and diligence files — and host management sessions.
    3. 33–5 LOIs
      Indicative bids
      Collect non-binding LOIs on price, structure and conditions against a published bid date.
    4. 42–3 short
      Down-select
      Shortlist on value, certainty and fit; give feedback and issue a refined process letter.
    5. 51–2 final
      Best-and-final
      Head-to-head rounds; negotiate the land lease and power JV on parallel tracks.
    6. 6Signed
      Confirm & close
      Confirmatory diligence, definitive agreements and signing — capital flows / the lease commences.
    Manufacture competition

    Compete the land lease and the power JV on separate tracks so each is bid on its own merits, and pit buyer classes — hyperscalers vs. neoclouds vs. IPP / JV partners — against one another. Running rounds in parallel keeps tension high and means we never anchor to a single bilateral offer.

    Why a process, not a phone call: a sealed, multi-party auction routinely clears 20–40% above the first unsolicited bid — and surfaces the structure (lease vs. JV vs. sale) that prices the asset highest.
    Engineer the cost of capital

    The cheapest capital follows the least risk. A signed, investment-grade anchor tenant + firm gas + a long-term offtake re-rates the project's risk and unlocks low-cost project finance. Pick the route that keeps the most upside:

    Ground lease to developerlowest · keep land
    Project finance vs. offtakedebt on contracted cash flow
    JV equity in power entityretain carry & upside
    Build alone (corp/dev equity)highest · only with signed tenant
    Trigger: a binding LOI with an investment-grade tenant or a developer fee (LandBridge's $8M ground-lease fee is a reference) → accelerate. Absent that, default to the low-risk long-term gas-supply route.
